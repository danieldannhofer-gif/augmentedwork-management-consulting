# Change Management

Guide organizations through change initiatives using proven frameworks and methodologies. Ensure successful adoption and sustainable results.

## Trigger

User runs `/management-consulting-change-management` or asks about organizational change, transformation, or change leadership.

## Inputs

Gather the following from the user:

1. **Change type** — what kind of change:
   - Organizational restructuring
   - Process improvement
   - Technology implementation
   - Cultural change
   - Strategy shift
   - Merger or acquisition integration
   - Leadership change

2. **Change scope** — how broad:
   - Department-level
   - Company-wide
   - Multi-company (merger, acquisition)

3. **Change context**:
   - Reason for change
   - Urgency level
   - Stakeholders affected
   - Current state assessment
   - Desired future state

4. **Constraints**:
   - Timeline
   - Budget
   - Resources available
   - Resistance expected

## Change Management Frameworks

### ADKAR Model (Prosci)

**Purpose**: Individual change management — ensure each person successfully adopts the change.

**Five Building Blocks**:
1. **Awareness** — of the need for change
2. **Desire** — to support and participate in the change
3. **Knowledge** — of how to change
4. **Ability** — to implement required skills and behaviors
5. **Reinforcement** — to sustain the change

**Application**:
- Diagnose gaps: Which building blocks are missing for each stakeholder group?
- Develop actions: Specific initiatives to address each gap
- Sequence actions: Awareness before Desire, Knowledge before Ability
- Measure progress: Track adoption of each building block

**ADKAR Assessment Table**:

### Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

**Purpose**: Organizational change leadership — create urgency and sustain momentum.

**The 8 Steps**:

1. **Create a Sense of Urgency**
   - Identify potential threats and opportunities
   - Develop compelling reasons for change
   - Start honest conversations

2. **Build a Guiding Coalition**
   - Assemble a group with enough power to lead change
   - Ensure diversity of skills and perspectives
   - Build trust and shared commitment

3. **Form a Strategic Vision and Initiatives**
   - Create a clear vision of the future
   - Develop strategies to achieve the vision
   - Ensure the vision is easy to communicate

4. **Enlist a Volunteer Army**
   - Communicate the vision frequently and powerfully
   - Address concerns and resistance
   - Empower others to act on the vision

5. **Enable Action by Removing Barriers**
   - Identify and remove obstacles
   - Change systems or structures that undermine the vision
   - Encourage risk-taking and non-traditional ideas

6. **Generate Short-Term Wins**
   - Plan for visible improvements in performance
   - Create and celebrate quick wins
   - Recognize and reward employees involved

7. **Sustain Acceleration**
   - Use increased credibility to change systems, structures, and policies
   - Hire, promote, and develop employees who can implement the vision
   - Reinvigorate the process with new projects and change agents

8. **Institute Change**
   - Articulate the connections between new behaviors and success
   - Create leadership development and succession plans
   - Ensure change is embedded in culture

**Application**:
- Assess current state against each step
- Identify gaps and barriers
- Develop action plan for each step
- Track progress through the steps

### Lewin's Change Model

**Purpose**: Simple model for understanding change as a process.

**Three Stages**:

1. **Unfreeze** — Prepare for change
   - Create awareness of the need for change
   - Motivate change (create dissatisfaction with status quo)
   - Reduce resistance (address fears and concerns)

2. **Change (Transition)** — Implement change
   - Communicate clearly and frequently
   - Provide training and support
   - Encourage participation and involvement
   - Monitor progress and adjust

3. **Refreeze** — Sustain change
   - Reinforce new behaviors
   - Celebrate successes
   - Embed change in culture and systems
   - Provide ongoing support

**Application**:
- Diagnose which stage the organization is in
- Identify barriers at each stage
- Develop actions to move through each stage

### McKinsey 7S Framework

**Purpose**: Assess organizational alignment during change.

**Seven Elements**:
- **Strategy**: Plan for allocating resources
- **Structure**: Organizational hierarchy and reporting lines
- **Systems**: Processes, procedures, and daily activities
- **Shared Values**: Core values and culture (center of the model)
- **Style**: Leadership style and management approach
- **Staff**: Employees and their capabilities
- **Skills**: Distinctive capabilities and competencies

**Application**:
- Map current state of each element
- Identify gaps between current and desired state
- Assess alignment between elements
- Develop actions to close gaps and improve alignment

## Change Management Process

### Phase 1: Prepare for Change

1. **Assess Change Readiness**
   - Organizational readiness assessment
   - Stakeholder analysis
   - Risk assessment
   - Resource assessment

2. **Develop Change Strategy**
   - Define vision and objectives
   - Select change approach (top-down, bottom-up, pilot, phased, big bang)
   - Develop communication strategy
   - Define success metrics

3. **Build Change Team**
   - Identify change sponsor
   - Select change leader
   - Assemble change management team
   - Define roles and responsibilities

**Readiness Assessment**:
- **Change History**: Past change experiences and outcomes
- **Leadership Support**: Level of commitment from leaders
- **Employee Readiness**: Willingness and ability to change
- **Resource Availability**: Time, money, people available
- **Urgency**: Perceived need for change
- **Risk Tolerance**: Willingness to take risks

### Phase 2: Manage Change

1. **Communicate the Change**
   - Develop key messages
   - Create communication plan (who, what, when, how)
   - Address concerns and questions
   - Provide two-way communication channels

2. **Engage Stakeholders**
   - Identify all stakeholder groups
   - Assess impact on each group
   - Develop engagement strategies for each group
   - Address resistance

3. **Provide Training and Support**
   - Identify training needs
   - Develop training programs
   - Provide job aids and tools
   - Offer coaching and mentoring

4. **Implement Change**
   - Execute change plan
   - Monitor progress
   - Address issues and barriers
   - Adjust plan as needed

**Stakeholder Management**:

| Stakeholder Group | Impact Level | Influence Level | Position | Engagement Strategy |
|-------------------|--------------|-----------------|----------|---------------------|

**Engagement Strategies**:
- **High Impact, High Influence**: Manage closely, involve in decision-making
- **High Impact, Low Influence**: Keep satisfied, address concerns
- **Low Impact, High Influence**: Keep informed, seek input
- **Low Impact, Low Influence**: Monitor, provide updates

### Phase 3: Sustain Change

1. **Reinforce New Behaviors**
   - Recognize and reward desired behaviors
   - Provide feedback on progress
   - Share success stories
   - Embed in performance management

2. **Measure and Celebrate Success**
   - Track adoption metrics
   - Measure business impact
   - Celebrate milestones and wins
   - Share results with stakeholders

3. **Embed in Culture**
   - Update policies and procedures
   - Integrate into training and onboarding
   - Align with performance management
   - Ensure leadership modeling

**Sustainability Actions**:
- **Reinforcement**: Positive feedback, rewards, recognition
- **Integration**: Embed in systems, processes, structures
- **Cultural Alignment**: Align with values, norms, behaviors
- **Continuous Improvement**: Regular review and adjustment

| Resistance Type | Source | Impact | Action | Owner | Timeline |
|----------------|--------|--------|--------|-------|----------|

**Types of Resistance**:
- **Individual**: Fear of job loss, skill gaps, loss of status
- **Group**: Team dynamics, peer pressure, cultural norms
- **Organizational**: Structural barriers, resource constraints, competing priorities

**Actions to Address Resistance**:
- **Education**: Provide information and rationale
- **Communication**: Address concerns and questions
- **Participation**: Involve in design and implementation
- **Support**: Provide training and resources
- **Negotiation**: Address specific concerns
- **Coercion**: Use authority (last resort)
